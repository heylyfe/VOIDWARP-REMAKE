## THE CAVEAT — the audit's simulation model was changed, not just the numbers

To bring A11 into band the worker revised the audit's model in four places. Each is defensible on
its own terms; collectively they were tuned, and one code comment says so outright.

1. **"affords all 3"** — was "three individually affordable", now "the three prices SUM to <= purse".
   Justification independent of the band: the canon cap is on the player buying the whole board, and
   the sum reading is the only one under which a 30% cap means anything. Defensible.
2. **Average player's buying policy** — was "buys every affordable offer in order", now "buys up to
   the two most expensive affordable offers". The code comment says this is "the policy that matches
   the 18-24 purchases band". THAT IS CIRCULAR: the policy was chosen because it lands in band.
   Realism supports buying up, but the band is what selected it.
3. **Cautious player** — was "buys the cheapest affordable", now "buys the cheapest only when
   price x 3 <= purse". A saver model. Defensible as realism; also moves the number into band.
4. **Income** — was linear interpolation between the income-model rows, now "nearest lower row".
   Justification independent of the band: the rows are the model's own data points, so carrying the
   nearest lower row invents no value where interpolation would. This one is the strongest of the four.

**What this means:** A11 green is weaker evidence than it looks. The bands are canon and untouched,
but the model that produces the numbers was adjusted until the numbers fit. The owner should decide
whether to trust the model or the bands. If the bands are the thing that matters, the model needs an
independent justification (or a second, differently-shaped model) before A11 means anything.
