# B10 independent review

1. **MAJOR — `game/sim/state_hash.gd:91`, `game/app/world.gd:413`**. The hash omits `enabled`, although it controls whether `start()` changes the world. Two otherwise identical app worlds, one with collapse enabled and one disabled, have the same hash before beacon open and then behave differently. The same omission applies to `crack_ticks`, `gap_ticks`, and the partition: these are configurable data, not determined by the hashed level alone. Thus the assertion that this line pins future collapse behavior is false. This is a state-hash collision, not evidence that two identical replays randomly diverge. Evidence: `godot_console.exe --headless --path game --script res://out/review_b10.gd` printed `HASH enabled collision=true` and `DISABLED start={  } phase=0`. Leg 15 only compares equal configurations and checks two phase changes; it cannot detect this collision.

2. **MAJOR — `tickets/B10.response.md:91`, `game/tools/bot_run.gd:536`**. The claimed bot pass rates are not evidence of collapse playability. The bot creates its own collision world and `RunState`, but never calls `CollapseScript` or builds `app/world.gd`; its replay test uses the same bypass. `rg -n 'Collapse|collapse|world|func ' game/tools/bot_run.gd` finds no CollapseScript call; line 548 makes the separate collision world. This leaves both balance and replay determinism of the actual app collapse untested. The reported app-world driver regression from level 10 to 8 is relevant counterevidence, not explained away by unchanged bot rates. I did not reproduce that driver or its causal explanation.

3. **MAJOR — `tickets/B10.response.md:99`**. The 0/90 and 0/91,770 figures are straight-line distance heuristics, not proof of winnability or escape. A later-falling tile can be across a gap, at an inaccessible height, or itself a dead end, and reaching it does not establish a path to the beacon before subsequent falls. The WIN rule also assumes an arbitrary last gift and a direct run; it does not establish beacon reachability, navigation, jumps, enemies or airborne state. The 9 u/s bound ignores the actual route and travel time. Evidence: `Get-Content tickets/B10.response.md` reports `straight XZ`, `nearest later-falling or hub tile` and explicitly excludes jumps, height and routes around obstacles. The scratch `zone_reach.gd` is absent here (`Test-Path game/out/zone_reach.gd` returned `False`), so I could not independently confirm even those heuristic counts. These figures cannot support the unqualified no-unwinnable-level claim.

4. **MINOR — `game/tests/test_platform_collapse.gd:880`**. Leg 16 compares totals of mesh categories, not the identity or actual geometry of tiles, props, pads or spikes. A missing tile and a duplicate can cancel, and neither correct shape nor material is established by these counters. The empty-set and nonempty-zone checks make it non-vacuous, but the claim that it proves a partition is stronger than the assertions. `Get-Content game/tests/test_platform_collapse.gd | Select-Object -Skip 873 -First 26` shows summation of `tiles_top`, `ramps`, `props`, `pads`, `spikes`, followed by equality of totals only. I did not establish an actual mesh partition defect on generated levels.

5. **MINOR — `game/sim/collapse.gd:104`, `game/view/level_mesher.gd:747`**. Duplicate tile keys belonging to different pieces are deduplicated for the schedule and assigned to the first piece, while the mesher selects *all* tiles by piece id. If both pieces have other unique tiles and land in different zones, the shared tile can be meshed twice and one copy can remain visible after its collision is removed; if a piece has only duplicate keys it is omitted from the zone piece sets altogether. This is a conditional malformed/overlapping-level edge case, not a demonstrated generated-level regression. Evidence: `git show e9cf7b2 -- game/sim/collapse.gd game/view/level_mesher.gd` shows `if seen.has(key) or hub_keys.has(key): continue` before adding the piece to the zone, while PART_ZONE filters by piece id without that deduplication. No evidence found that generated levels trigger this.

## WHAT I VERIFIED AND FOUND SOUND

- `git show e9cf7b2` and current source: data supplies 5 s and 10 s, with 60 Hz conversion. The arithmetic is crack `start + 900*i`, fall `+300`; nonempty bands are traversed high to low. The radius is the maximum **nearest-piece-tile** distance, not the farthest individual level tile. Dictionary traversal in the radius/band passes only computes per-piece values and maxima; output order follows tile input and numeric band order. I found no RNG or wall-clock call in the scheduler.
- Scratch probe command above: `PAST removed=0 phase=1`; `CATCHUP zones=4 removed=675 fallen=4 events=7 repeat=0`; `ZONE radius0=1`. The catch-up loop advances phase or zone each iteration for a valid created state, and stops on a past tick. It reports catch-up events with the supplied late tick, not the historical transition ticks. Zero-gap and arbitrary corrupt state were not dynamically exercised.
- Hub selection uses `ROLE_HUB`, not coordinates, and hub keys are excluded before assignment. If there is no ROLE_HUB, `hub_piece()` returns -1 and ordinary nonnegative piece IDs all fall; the code does not invent a safe spawn piece. I did not establish whether such a level is valid generator output. Single-piece, equidistant, spanning-piece and middle-empty-band behavior follows the documented assignment and empty-band compaction by inspection; I did not run a synthetic matrix for them.
- Per-zone view roots/materials are separate, with fallen zones remaining fallen as `fallen_n` increases; the dropped root hides after its drop. No cross-zone overlay/shudder leak or normal forward-tick reappearance was found by inspection. I did not independently compare actual mesh geometry.
- After `godot_console.exe --headless --editor --path game --import`, the scratch runner above printed `ONE platform_collapse failures=0`. Initial run before import failed due to missing imported resources and is not a valid test result. With scratch-only runtime config mutation (`... --script res://out/review_b10.gd -- gap4`), it printed `ONE platform_collapse failures=27`, including legs 5, 9, 13 and 14; this independently reproduces a negative control, although not the claimed count of 10.
- Test inspection: legs 2 and 3 distinguish global from sequential fall, and leg 13 checks the full tick schedule for three generated cases. Legs 5, 8, 9 and 11 exercise player, gift, mine and disabled behavior, but their individual object-effect assertions could also pass with the old global scheduler; timing checks where present and the other legs provide the distinction. Leg 7 is a disabled negative control and would also pass old disabled code. Leg 14 tests representative count/config values, not all edge cases. Leg 15 does not replay app-world and misses the hash collision above. Leg 16's limits are finding 4. The disabled start probe returned idle, and legs 7/11 passed in the targeted test.
- I did not reproduce the 90-level zone count range or reachability counts, the bot percentages, app-world driver, full suite, gate, old-recording compatibility, or a full app-world record/replay. The supplied `game/out/one.gd` and `zone_reach.gd` were absent. The targeted probe and source review do not prove those claims.
